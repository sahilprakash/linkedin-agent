## Idea 2 — Power BI & DAX
**Topic:** Calculated Columns vs Measures: When to Use Each

**Hook:** Calculated column or measure—what’s the real difference?

**Full Draft:**

Calculated column or measure—what’s the real difference?

I’ve seen a lot of teams over‑use calculated columns just because they’re easier to drag into visuals. But a calculated column forces a row‑by‑row calculation, bloating the model. Measures, on the other hand, compute on demand, respecting the current filter context.

A practical rule: use a calculated column when the value is static per row (e.g., a fiscal quarter for a transaction). Use a measure when you need flexibility across different slicers or when the value depends on the report’s context.

In a recent project, switching from a calculated column to a measure cut the model size by 40% and eliminated a long lag when adding a new slicer.

Share a scenario where you chose a calculated column over a measure—what was the trade‑off?

## Idea 3 — Power BI & DAX
**Topic:** Mastering CALCULATE: 3 Unexpected Filters

**Hook:** CALCULATE can be a wizard—watch it work.

**Full Draft:**

CALCULATE can be a wizard—watch it work.

I discovered three filter tricks that turn CALCULATE into a lean, mean data engine.

1. **ALL** removes all filters from a table, giving you the grand total behind the scenes.
2. **ALLEXCEPT** keeps filters on selected columns while stripping everything else—great for year‑over‑year comparisons.
3. **REMOVEFILTERS** is the cleanest way to reset filters on specific columns without touching the rest.

In one report I needed the current quarter’s sales compared to the same quarter last year. By combining ALLEXCEPT with a DATEADD inside CALCULATE, the measure returned the correct relative value in under a millisecond.

Give these patterns a try in your next dashboard—do you see a performance boost? Let me know what you discover.
