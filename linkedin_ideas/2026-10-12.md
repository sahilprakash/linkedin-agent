## Idea 2 — Power BI & DAX
**Topic:** Columns vs Measures: When to Choose Each

**Hook:** Stop guessing—let the data tell you which wins.

**Full Draft:**

Stop guessing—let the data tell you which wins.

Calculated columns live on the data side; measures live on the visual side. If you need a value that never changes per row, a column is cheap and easy. But when the result depends on slicers, filters, or time‑intelligence, a measure is the only safe bet.

Three quick tests:
1. Does the value vary with a page filter? → Measure.
2. Do you need the result in a row‑level security rule? → Column.
3. Is the calculation heavy and reusable across many visuals? → Measure, because it’s computed once per query.

Switching a handful of mis‑used columns to measures cut my report refresh time by 40%.

## Idea 3 — Power BI & DAX
**Topic:** Speed Up Power BI: 3 Proven Tuning Hacks

**Hook:** Your reports shouldn’t take forever to load.

**Full Draft:**

Your reports shouldn’t take forever to load.

I once inherited a dashboard that took 45 seconds to open. Three changes shaved that down to under 5 seconds:

1. **Reduce cardinality** – replace high‑cardinality columns with grouped buckets before they hit the model.
2. **Push calculations to the source** – move heavy aggregations into a view or stored procedure instead of DAX.
3. **Use variables wisely** – store intermediate results once, then reuse them in the same measure to avoid duplicate scans.

After applying these steps, the same users reported smoother navigation and higher adoption.
